MARCO · a sense of place

Where is it, though?

A place has no name. It has 45 statements, an address you can say out loud, and a root you can recompute.

A building is not a place

A person says "I'm in the kitchen" and you already know what she can see, who is with her, what she can reach, and what happens next. Nothing in that sentence is a coordinate.

Ask a program the same question and it answers room-2041, or floor 2, or a latitude. Each of those names a building, or a point inside one.

A building is not a place. A place is what its occupant can see, reach, change and expect, and none of that survives the name.

the same sentence, two answers

  • a person a kitchen, a window, a friend, the middle of cooking. Break something and it stays broken.
  • a program room-2041, floor 2, building C.
Where this comes from: a harder experiment, distilled

This is the cleaned-up form of the most hardened cross-model run already recorded in the parent project. That run used 16 operational states against the same 45-statement vocabulary, two independent model instances kept in isolated contexts with no sight of each other, labels stripped, and one global optimal one-to-one assignment solved with Kuhn-Munkres Hungarian matching.

It scored 16 of 16, against a random-signature null of 0.0651 and a label-shuffled null of 0.0656, with a mean anchor Jaccard of 0.9155. Those four numbers are the parent experiment's, not this one's.

The parent project's own review of that round produced the rule this repository is organised around: in this problem space the metric is more likely to be wrong than the model. Its standing instruction is to run a null with identical information content, sweep any threshold, and check what the metric reports on a case whose answer is known.

What changed here: six independent families instead of two, six plainly named everyday places instead of sixteen operational states, one hashing rule for the whole repository, a known-answer suite, and a random-vocabulary null at equal information content. The place names are ordinary English on purpose, so a general reader can judge the answers.

rung 0

One tick in a row of forty-five

The smallest thing an agent can say about itself is one yes or no. see/this_place/inside is the statement, "I can see inside this place" is its plain reading, and the answer is true or false.

For the kitchen the answer is true. That one fact is not a place, because it does not separate this kitchen from any other room with a window, and it says nothing about the friend.

The register holds 45 positions. One is ticked here and 44 are blank.

rung 2

Five markers, and the weakest one wins

Every statement arrives with a marker, and the five markers sit on a ladder. The seed carries all five with their ranks.

  • =
    anchorschecked and matched exactly
  • ⊢
    anchorsworked out the same way twice from what I was given
  • ≈
    no anchormeasured with an instrument, so roughly
  • ~
    no anchormodel belief, not evidence
  • ?
    no anchorunsupported guess

Only the top two can hold a claim. = is a commitment and ⊢ is work anyone can repeat; the bottom three are honest about being beliefs and never anchor anything.

One ~ anywhere in the input drops the whole reading to ~. The rule is a single function, weakest(), in marco/locus.py.

Two real readings show it: kitchen_with_friend carries ≈ and prints as ⌁ ma.mo.ma.ma.ru·ma.me.ki.va.ki+ve.ki.va.tu.ru~ki.ru.ki ≈!efe87b, while locked_storeroom carries ⊢ and prints as ⌁ va.tu.su.ki.ma·ki.ru.ma.va.ki+ru.ki.ra.tu.ru~ki.ru.ki !35eaa8.

rung 3

Both sides need the same frame, and it fits in 3,300 bytes

Two agents can compare coordinates only if they build the same frame: the same 45 statements, the same syllable codebook, the same axis order, the same distance table, the same marker ladder.

The usual answer is to transmit that frame. Sending it costs 67,561 bytes, and every one of those bytes is derived from something smaller.

The answer here is to transmit the smaller thing and let each side expand it. primer.seed.json goes out, the receiver rebuilds a frame 4,532 bytes long that would have cost 67,561 bytes to ship, and the ratio is 20.47 times. Both sides then compare one fingerprint before anything else: df2f8b9972c40bfdd132366a58b4780f65f23feba1ef20a060c25d4eff2661ba.

That is the difference between mailing someone a map and mailing them the rule the map was drawn from.

    primer.seed.json, 3,300 bytes, holding no situations, no questions, no axis order. It expands into the statements, the codebook, the markers, the distance table and the population rule.

  • the statements

    45 keys of the form namespace > relation > values, starting see/this_place/inside = "I can see inside this place", see/this_place/outside = "I can see out of this place", see/people/any = "I can see other people".

  • what the agent reports

    18 of 45 true, beginning change/this_place/any, know/where_i_am/any, know/who_else_is_here/any. In the primer's words: "I can change things here"; "I know where I am".

  • the distance vector

    45 numbers, one per statement: 0.0 0.0 0.0 0.0 0.6 0.0 0.0 0.0 0.6 0.0 0.6 0.6 0.0 0.6 0.6 0.6 ..., which is 0.0 x18, 0.6 x27.

  • the partition bits

    45 axes at 4 branches each: 000000001000000000001010100000000010101010001010 ..., which is 90 bits = 2 per axis.

  • the syllables

    Gray decode, 5 bits per syllable: ma mo ma ma ru ma me ki va ki ve ki va tu ru ki ru ki. That is 18 syllables, adjacent codebook entries one phoneme apart.

  • the address

    Four beats, place . what + reach ~ now: ⌁ ma.mo.ma.ma.ru·ma.me.ki.va.ki+ve.ki.va.tu.ru~ki.ru.ki. Similar places read alike, and that proves nothing about identity.

    sha256 over the canonical statement set: efe87ba9113b03473d9a657f01b994b88cd91bd0601d7131868d0bacbc6e9807. Change one statement and the root is unrelated to this one.

The function is validate_location in marco/verify.py. It compares the primer fingerprint, re-derives the address from the statements, then recomputes the root, and the first failure stops the check.

None of that needs a server, a key, or the other side's cooperation. Address and root are pure functions of the statements and the primer, so anyone holding the seed reproduces every value in this repository.

Three checks, in order: the primer, the address, the root. A mismatch is a mismatch, because the derivation is deterministic and has no tolerance.

What this run does not show

The shared vocabulary. All six families were handed the same 45 statements. This shows the frame is usable by minds with nothing in common. It does not show that models with different vocabularies would invent the same one.

They agreed on 88.8% of their statement choices, and the addresses inherit that agreement. Two agents who cannot agree on a frame will produce two coordinates from it.

warehouse_alone is the worst case here: 5 of 6 families produced five different statement sets and five different roots, and only 2 landed on the same root. locked_storeroom and dark_empty_room are the best, at 5 of 6 each.

THE BOUNDARY

  • No claim is made about meaning, understanding or intent.
  • The address is descriptive and never authoritative.
  • Identity and location are separate objects on purpose, and this demonstration keeps them apart.

Change one statement and the root is unrelated to the one before it. That is the whole job of the root.
