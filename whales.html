Can a whale phrase be given an address?

A coda is written down as 5R and a neural response as a spike rate, and the two descriptions share no frame. Nothing here decodes a whale.

established contested speculative

Two descriptions of one sound share no frame

A field biologist describes a sperm-whale coda as 5R or 1+1+3 and reports its tempo and its inter-click intervals. A neurophysiologist describes the response to that same sound as a spike rate, a latency, a trajectory through population activity, or a BOLD change.

The two vocabularies name different observables, so no conversion between them is available on either side. That is the problem the rest of this repository worked on, with 45 statements, a 3,300-byte seed and one derived frame.

the same sound, two descriptions

  • behaviour 5R, 1+1+3, tempo, inter-click intervals, and a behavioural context.
  • physiology spike rate, latency, a trajectory through population activity, a BOLD change.

45 statements · 1 frame · 2 vocabularies with no mapping between them

The analogy is structural, not semantic

In this repository an agent declares landmarks about itself, and one frame converts those declarations into distances, a coarse-to-fine address of 18 syllables, and a separate exact identity of 32 bytes. The separation of those objects is implemented and measured here.

For whale work the recording supplies measurable acoustic features where the declarations were, and neural activity supplies a second observation space. The bridge is a learned correspondence between the two spaces rather than a shared dictionary, and the figure further down draws it stage by stage with a control hanging off every stage.

established the separation of a descriptive address from an exact identity is implemented and measured in this repository. speculative that it is useful for cetacean neurobiology.

A descriptive address

It preserves neighbourhood, so near phrases land near each other. Measured here at Spearman rho 0.9286 over all 15 pairs of six places, against a shuffled null of +0.0002 with a standard deviation of 0.2704.

An exact root

SHA-256 over a canonical set. The same set gives the same 32 bytes, and one statement different gives unrelated bytes, so the root destroys adjacency deliberately. The address and the root are built from the same 45 statements and behave in opposite ways.

A prediction

An empirical estimate of a measured response, scored on held-out phrases. It is not a decoded message, and it is not an address.

Neural decoding exists for four other animals

The contrast is what bounds the claim, so it belongs on the page before any of the machinery. Bat, zebra finch, marmoset and macaque each have a published single-unit or imaging result, and the cetacean row has none of them.

Four animals, one absence

animalwhat has been measured
batsingle-unit auditory-cortex neurons tuned to echo delay and velocity, yielding range-like maps
zebra finchsingle-unit auditory-forebrain responses encode conspecific song and tutor familiarity
marmosetsingle-unit A1 activity tracks self-generated vocal feedback (Eliades & Wang 2008, Journal of Neuroscience)
macaquevoice-selective temporal cortex, fMRI and single-unit (Petkov et al. 2008, Neuron)
cetaceanno chronic single-unit or ECoG recordings; evoked potentials and psychoacoustics only

Cetacean neural data of this kind does not exist, because ethics, permits, animal size, immersion and cetacean neuroanatomy make the bat, songbird and marmoset approach unavailable. Non-invasive auditory evoked potentials and behavioural psychoacoustics do exist, and for this question that is the ceiling.

Any claim of a whale phrase-to-neuron map is speculative until a lawful, interpretable neural measurement exists. The four animals above are the whole of the evidence base.

4 animals with single-unit or imaging data · 1 with none · 2 non-invasive methods as the ceiling

The bridge, built in five steps

Every step below is a quantity someone can measure or a search someone can bound. Nothing in it needs a whale to have a language.

01

A phrase vector of 18 dimensions, from a calibrated recording

Dimension 1 is unit count: clicks for a coda, segmented syllables or events for a phrase. Dimensions 2 to 6 are the first five inter-unit intervals, in milliseconds and normalised by phrase duration, with shorter phrases padded by an explicit missing-value mask. Dimension 7 is median tempo in events per second.

Dimensions 8 to 10 are rubato: mean signed deviation from a nominal timing grid, its standard deviation, and the maximum absolute deviation. Dimensions 11 to 14 are spectral-centroid trajectory at four equally spaced temporal landmarks, 15 to 16 are bandwidth at an early and a late window, and 17 to 18 are ornamentation flags or scores. That is 1 + 5 + 1 + 3 + 4 + 2 + 2 = 18.

established these are measurable acoustic quantities. speculative that this 18-dimensional representation is the biologically relevant one, because microphone placement, propagation, overlap and detector error can dominate small differences.

02

Spectral anchors replace the hand-declared statements

The demonstration's 45 plain statements become a fixed set of K = 64 spectral anchors, each a reference phrase or reference template in the standardised feature space.

They are chosen before any neural fitting, by k-means++ over a training corpus repeated 100 times with the lowest held-out distortion retained, or by hand-picked exemplars spanning unit count, tempo, timing shape and ornamentation, and the exact exemplars and preprocessing parameters are stored.

For phrase vector x and anchor a_k, the distance d_k is scaled Euclidean for continuous features, dynamic-time-warping for trajectories and Hamming for flags, weighted only inside training folds, then quantised into four bins analogous to 0.0, 0.3, 0.6, 0.9 with thresholds calibrated on training data. contested no single metric is justified in advance.

03

The same coarse-to-fine partition gives a phrase address

The 64-distance vector is partitioned hierarchically at 4 branches per axis, which is 64 × 2 = 128 bits before any rendering, with axes ordered by discriminative power measured only on training folds. The order matters more here than in the demonstration: the first axis should probably be unit count or tempo rather than spectral centroid, and that is a hypothesis to test rather than a result.

The quantised path is Gray-decoded into the same 32-entry adjacent-phoneme codebook on the same one-phoneme-per-bit principle, so the address is a stable neighbourhood label and not a name for a behavioural category.

04

The predictive hypothesis, stated formally

Let q(x) be the phrase address as a real-valued bit or branch vector after calibration, and let y be a neural response vector, binned firing rates, latency features, or a low-dimensional population trajectory. The primary hypothesis is that there exist W and b such that prediction on held-out phrases exceeds a preregistered null and generalises across recording sessions, individuals and contexts.

Nonlinear extensions are tested only after this linear baseline. A successful W would mean the address preserves information useful for predicting a measured response; it would not mean the address is a neural code.

05

A deliberately broad brute-force search, with its size stated

Roughly 10³ candidate anchor sets after clustering and exemplar sampling, 6 feature metrics, 5 quantisation schemes, 4 axis-ordering rules and 3 response alignments give 10³ × 6 × 5 × 4 × 3 = 360,000 topological candidates. Fitting W by ridge regression over 20 logarithmically spaced penalties gives 360,000 × 20 = 7,200,000 fitted candidates before permutations, plus CCA and, where dimensions are matched, orthogonal Procrustes.

The objective is held-out neural prediction, cross-validated correlation, mean-squared error and representational dissimilarity alignment, combined only after a predeclared primary metric. Inner folds select anchors, metrics, quantisation, axis order and penalty, and outer folds estimate performance on phrases and sessions never used for selection.

A candidate that wins 7.2 million trials by chance has not discovered a map. The search is that large on purpose, and the null has to be that large with it.

The pipeline, and where each control attaches

Six stages take one calibrated recording to a prediction of a held-out response, and six controls hang off the stages they can corrupt. The phrase address is stage four and the prediction is stage six, so nothing downstream can be scored before the address is frozen.

The controls, which are the hard part

The canonical tools are long established: canonical correlation analysis (CCA) originates with Hotelling (1936), orthogonal Procrustes gives a constrained alignment (Schönemann 1966, Psychometrika, doi:10.1007/BF02289451), representational similarity analysis compares dissimilarity structures instead of pretending that coordinates are commensurate (Kriegeskorte et al. 2008, Frontiers in Human Neuroscience, doi:10.3389/neuro.01.1.1.004.2008), encoding models predict neural measurements from stimulus features (Naselaris et al. 2011, NeuroImage, doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2010.07.073), and semantic encoding maps show both the ambition and the assumptions (Huth et al. 2016, Nature, doi:10.1038/nature17637).

Circular analysis is the same error in another form, selecting features because they fit the neural data and then evaluating them on those same data (Kriegeskorte et al. 2009, Nature Neuroscience, doi:10.1038/nn.2303). The address is frozen before the test fold, and neural preprocessing never uses a held-out label.

7 controls · 1 search of 360,000 topologies · 6,000 permutations per null

Sample-size collapse. CCA can produce near-perfect in-sample correlation when predictor and response dimensions approach the number of samples. A few hundred phrases against a population response containing thousands of neurons is exactly that regime, and flexible projections can align noise after a search over anchors, metrics, alignments and penalties. Dimensionality reduction does not cure it automatically: if the reduction is selected using all trials, the leakage merely moves upstream.

The governing rule is severe: in a space like this the metric is more likely to be wrong than the model, and any reported mapping must survive a null with identical information content. A high correlation, a clean visual trajectory or an intelligible phonetic address is not a control.

The real result against each null

Every family was placed against every other family for every place, which is 90 comparisons, and the same place came back nearest in all 90 of them. Counting exact ties, the score is 100.0 per cent; strictly, it is 96.7 per cent against a chance of 16.7 per cent, which is 1 in 6.

Shuffling the phrase labels scores 16.9 per cent with a standard deviation of 16.8 over 6,000 trials. A random vocabulary of equal size, which replaces each family's selection with a random set of the same size drawn from the same 45 statements, scores 20.9 per cent with a standard deviation of 16.5 over the same 6,000 trials.

Six settings, each with the result that would end it

Every rung below names the measurement it uses and the single result that ends it. None of them is tested on an animal whose neural response cannot be measured, which is why the cetacean rungs stop where they do.

00 · synthetic phrase spaces, no whales

Phrase spaces carry known anchors, a known axis order, known quantisation and a known W. Falsifier: the pipeline cannot recover W, or its out-of-sample performance collapses under realistic noise. This rung is where the failure is cheapest to find, so it varies sample size until the CCA failure becomes visible.

01 · bird song, where signal and neural response both exist

Zebra-finch song features against auditory-forebrain responses, with tutor familiarity as a known behavioural and neural contrast. Falsifier: the method fails to distinguish known song classes, or cannot beat a simple spectrotemporal baseline under held-out birds and sessions. This tests the whole measurement-to-neural pipeline where recordings exist.

02 · bat echolocation, where the physical variable is known

Echolocation parameters against auditory-cortex single-unit responses, including delay and velocity tuning. Falsifier: a topology of this kind performs no better than shuffled addresses, or cannot recover a range-like response manifold. This tests whether anchors and coarse-to-fine order preserve a known physical variable.

03 · marmoset vocal feedback

Self-generated vocalisations against A1 activity, in the Eliades & Wang 2008 setting. Falsifier: no held-out prediction beyond acoustic energy, onset and motor-corollary baselines. This tests active feedback rather than passive stimulus response.

04 · cetacean behaviour, without neurons

Stable coda and song addresses built from independently recorded populations, tested on identity, context, overlap, matching, cultural change and cross-site stability. Falsifier: addresses fail to replicate under microphone, recorder, location or individual hold-out. This is useful topology engineering, and it is not a neural map.

05 · non-invasive cetacean physiology, if it is ever justified

Controlled acoustic presentation paired with auditory evoked potentials or other permitted non-invasive measures. Falsifier: no reproducible phrase-conditioned response after correcting for loudness, timing, movement and subject identity. Even success would establish prediction of a measured evoked signal, not cortical semantics.

At every rung the address has to beat raw acoustic features, a standard spectrogram embedding, a simple timing model and a randomly generated topology carrying equal information. That comparison is the whole test of whether the topology earns its complexity.

6 rungs · 1 falsifier each · 4 baselines to beat at every rung

What this cannot become

Nothing in this proposal can establish that a coda or a song phrase has meaning. A predictive acoustic-to-neural map would not identify a referent, an intention or a semantic composition; it would be an encoding model, signal in and measured response out, and a good predictor is not a dictionary.

A locus, a phrase address or a neural coordinate is descriptive and never authoritative. In a whale application, a highly repeatable acoustic feature must not be promoted to a behavioural landmark without independent evidence, and an inferred neural association stays a hypothesis until it survives held-out subjects, temporal shuffles, decoys and search-corrected nulls.

The strongest defensible outcome is modest: a reproducible, topology-preserving description of vocal signals, plus a rigorously tested prediction of a particular neural measurement where such data exist. For cetaceans today the first object is feasible and the second is not established.

Why six independent systems could agree at all

Handing six systems the same 45 statements does not explain the result. A shared vocabulary tells you what to answer with; it does not make two differently built systems put the same situation in the same part of their own space. If that were all that was happening, the addresses would be arbitrary but consistent, like two people agreeing to call a colour "seven".

What the agreement implies is stronger. Situations appear to occupy a structure that independently trained systems have in common, and a chart laid over that structure is what the primer is, rather than a substitute for it.

The two halves are different kinds of object. The manifold is shared, emergent and not ours: we did not build it, we cannot inspect it directly, and its existence is inferred from the fact that independent systems agree. The chart is ours, published and small: 45 statements, a 32-syllable codebook, an axis order derived over 204 places, one hashing rule, 3,300 bytes.

The shared-vocabulary caveat, and nothing here removes it

The shared-vocabulary caveat is inherited, not discovered here. The parent experiment's handover report states it first: its blind cross-model matching used a shared anchor vocabulary and a deterministic encoder, so it demonstrates encoding-level matching given a shared primer. It does not establish independence from a shared ontology. Its recommended follow-up, a genuine-versus-shuffled-anchors-versus-random-loci-versus-garbage study, has still not been run.

The parent project also set out the falsifier. Independence from a shared ontology is shown only when genuine anchors beat shuffled anchors, syntax-valid random loci, and an empty or garbage condition, not when a shared-label run scores well. This repository restates the caveat because it still applies and nothing here removes it.

That narrows the caveat without removing it. The families still chose from a vocabulary we handed them, so what is shown is that the frame is usable by minds with nothing in common, not that minds with different vocabularies would invent the same one.

They agreed on 88.8% of their statement choices, and the addresses inherit that agreement. The warehouse alone is the worst case: 5 of 6 families produced five different statement sets and five different roots, and only 2 landed on the same root. The locked storeroom and the dark empty room are the best, at 5 of 6 each.

No claim is made about meaning, understanding or intent. The address is descriptive and never authoritative, and identity and location are separate objects on purpose.
