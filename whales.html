Can a whale phrase be given an address?

Whale structure is described in a behavioural vocabulary, 5R, 1+1+3, tempo, inter-click intervals, and a neural response is described in a physiological one, spike rate, latency, a trajectory through population activity, a BOLD change. These are valid descriptions of different observables with no shared frame, which is the problem the rest of this repository just worked on.

What follows keeps three labels apart throughout: established contested speculative. Nothing here decodes a whale, and nothing here claims that a coda or a song phrase has a meaning.

The analogy is structural, not semantic

In this repository an agent declares landmarks about itself, and a shared frame converts those declarations into distances, a coarse-to-fine address of 18 syllables, and a separate exact identity of 32 bytes. For whale work the recording supplies measurable acoustic features instead of declarations, and neural activity supplies a second observation space, so the proposed bridge is a learned correspondence between the two spaces rather than a shared dictionary. Established: that separation of objects is implemented and measured in this repository. Speculative: that it will be useful for cetacean neurobiology.

Three objects, three jobs
kept apart on purpose

A descriptive address. Preserves neighbourhood: near phrases land near each other. Measured here at Spearman rho 0.9286 over all 15 pairs of six places, against a shuffled null of +0.0002 (sd 0.2704).

An exact root. SHA-256 over a canonical set. Same set, same 32 bytes; one statement different, unrelated bytes. It destroys adjacency deliberately.

A prediction. An empirical estimate of a measured response. It is not a decoded message, and it is not an address.

Neural decoding exists for four other animals

The contrast is what bounds the proposal, so it belongs on the page before any of the machinery. Cetacean neural data of this kind does not exist: there are no chronic implanted single-unit or ECoG recordings in wild cetaceans, because ethics, permits, animal size, immersion and cetacean neuroanatomy make the bat, songbird and marmoset approach unavailable.

Non-invasive auditory evoked potentials and behavioural psychoacoustics do exist, and for the question proposed here that is the ceiling. Any claim of a whale phrase-to-neuron map is therefore speculative until a lawful, interpretable neural measurement exists.

Four animals, one absence
batsingle-unit auditory-cortex neurons tuned to echo delay and velocity, yielding range-like maps
zebra finchsingle-unit auditory-forebrain responses encode conspecific song and tutor familiarity
marmosetsingle-unit A1 activity tracks self-generated vocal feedback (Eliades & Wang 2008, Journal of Neuroscience)
macaquevoice-selective temporal cortex, fMRI and single-unit (Petkov et al. 2008, Neuron)
cetaceanno chronic single-unit or ECoG recordings; evoked potentials and psychoacoustics only

The bridge, built in five steps

Every step below is a quantity someone can measure or a search someone can bound. Nothing in it needs a whale to have a language.

01
A phrase vector of 18 dimensions, from a calibrated recording
Dimension 1 is unit count: clicks for a coda, segmented syllables or events for a phrase. Dimensions 2, 6 are the first five inter-unit intervals, in milliseconds and normalised by phrase duration, with shorter phrases padded by an explicit missing-value mask. Dimension 7 is median tempo in events per second. Dimensions 8, 10 are rubato: mean signed deviation from a nominal timing grid, its standard deviation, and the maximum absolute deviation. Dimensions 11, 14 are spectral-centroid trajectory at four equally spaced temporal landmarks; 15, 16 are bandwidth at an early and a late window; 17, 18 are ornamentation flags or scores. That is 1 + 5 + 1 + 3 + 4 + 2 + 2 = 18. Established: these are measurable acoustic quantities. Speculative: that this 18-dimensional representation is the biologically relevant one, because microphone placement, propagation, overlap and detector error can dominate small differences.
02
Spectral anchors replace the hand-declared statements
The demonstration's 45 plain statements become a fixed set of K = 64 spectral anchors: each a reference phrase or reference template in the standardised feature space. They are chosen before any neural fitting, by k-means++ over a training corpus repeated 100 times with the lowest held-out distortion retained, or by hand-picked exemplars spanning unit count, tempo, timing shape and ornamentation; the exact exemplars and preprocessing parameters are stored. For phrase vector x and anchor a_k, the distance d_k is scaled Euclidean for continuous features, dynamic-time-warping for trajectories and Hamming for flags, weighted only inside training folds, then quantised into four bins analogous to 0.0, 0.3, 0.6, 0.9 with thresholds calibrated on training data. Contested: no single metric is justified in advance.
03
The same coarse-to-fine partition gives a phrase address
The 64-distance vector is partitioned hierarchically at 4 branches per axis, which is 64 × 2 = 128 bits before any rendering, with axes ordered by discriminative power measured only on training folds. The order matters more here than in the demonstration: the first axis should probably be unit count or tempo rather than spectral centroid, and that is a hypothesis to test rather than a result. The quantised path is Gray-decoded into the same 32-entry adjacent-phoneme codebook on the same one-phoneme-per-bit principle, so the address is a stable neighbourhood label and not a name for a behavioural category.
04
The predictive hypothesis, stated formally
Let q(x) be the phrase address as a real-valued bit or branch vector after calibration, and let y be a neural response vector, binned firing rates, latency features, or a low-dimensional population trajectory. The primary hypothesis is that there exist W and b such that ŷ = W q(x) + b, where prediction on held-out phrases exceeds a preregistered null and generalises across recording sessions, individuals and contexts. Nonlinear extensions are tested only after this linear baseline. A successful W would mean the address preserves information useful for predicting a measured response; it would not mean the address is a neural code. speculative hypothesis, with no cetacean neural data behind it
05
A deliberately broad brute-force search, with its size stated
Roughly 10³ candidate anchor sets after clustering and exemplar sampling, 6 feature metrics, 5 quantisation schemes, 4 axis-ordering rules and 3 response alignments give 10³ × 6 × 5 × 4 × 3 = 360,000 topological candidates. Fitting W by ridge regression over 20 logarithmically spaced penalties gives 360,000 × 20 = 7,200,000 fitted candidates before permutations, plus CCA and, where dimensions are matched, orthogonal Procrustes. The objective is held-out neural prediction, cross-validated correlation, mean-squared error and representational dissimilarity alignment, combined only after a predeclared primary metric. Inner folds select anchors, metrics, quantisation, axis order and penalty; outer folds estimate performance on phrases and sessions never used for selection. A candidate that wins 7.2 million trials by chance has not discovered a map.

The controls, which are the hard part

The canonical tools are long established: canonical correlation analysis (CCA) originates with Hotelling (1936), orthogonal Procrustes gives a constrained alignment (Schönemann 1966, Psychometrika, doi:10.1007/BF02289451), representational similarity analysis compares dissimilarity structures instead of pretending that coordinates are commensurate (Kriegeskorte et al. 2008, Frontiers in Human Neuroscience, doi:10.3389/neuro.01.1.1.004.2008), encoding models predict neural measurements from stimulus features (Naselaris et al. 2011, NeuroImage, doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2010.07.073), and semantic encoding maps show both the ambition and the assumptions (Huth et al. 2016, Nature, doi:10.1038/nature17637). Circular analysis is the same error in another form, selecting features because they fit the neural data and then evaluating them on those same data (Kriegeskorte et al. 2009, Nature Neuroscience, doi:10.1038/nn.2303).

controlwhat it rules out
Permutation null over phrase labelsRandomly reassign phrase identities to neural windows, rerun the complete search, and keep the maximum score per permutation. The null must include the same 360,000-candidate topology search.
Temporal-alignment shuffleShift or permute the phrase relative to the neural window while preserving autocorrelation and trial counts. A mapping that survives arbitrary misalignment is probably exploiting session structure.
Cross-validated held-out phrasesReport only outer-fold predictions, with held-out individuals or sessions where possible. In-sample CCA correlation is never evidence.
A known-answer caseGenerate synthetic phrase vectors, choose a hand-built W with known noise, and confirm recovery, calibration and the expected degradation as noise rises.
A decoyPair the phrase set with an unrelated neural dataset and require the pipeline to report no mapping above the corrected null.
Held-out anchorsFit anchors on one corpus partition and evaluate addresses using anchors never selected from the test phrases.
Multiplicity correctionCorrect across the whole candidate topology and model search, preferably by maximum-statistic permutation or a preregistered family-wise procedure.
Sample-size collapse
CCA can produce near-perfect in-sample correlation when predictor and response dimensions approach the number of samples. A few hundred phrases against a population response containing thousands of neurons is exactly that regime, and flexible projections can align noise after a search over anchors, metrics, alignments and penalties. Dimensionality reduction does not cure it automatically: if the reduction is selected using all trials, the leakage merely moves upstream.

The governing rule is severe: in a space like this the metric is more likely to be wrong than the model, and any reported mapping must survive a null with identical information content. A high correlation, a clean visual trajectory or an intelligible phonetic address is not a control.

Milestones, each with its falsifier written next to it

A proposal without a falsifier is a hope, so every rung below names the result that ends it. The order matters: the mapping is never tested on an animal whose neural response cannot be measured.

00
Synthetic phrase spaces, no whales
Build phrase spaces with known anchors, a known axis order, known quantisation and a known W. Falsifier: the pipeline cannot recover W, or its out-of-sample performance collapses under realistic noise. Vary sample size until the CCA failure becomes visible and publish the curve, because this rung is where the failure is cheapest to find.
01
Bird song, where signal and neural response both exist
Zebra-finch song features against auditory-forebrain responses, with tutor familiarity as a known behavioural and neural contrast. Falsifier: the method fails to distinguish known song classes, or cannot beat a simple spectrotemporal baseline under held-out birds and sessions. This tests the whole measurement-to-neural pipeline where recordings exist.
02
Bat echolocation, where the physical variable is known
Echolocation parameters against auditory-cortex single-unit responses, including delay and velocity tuning. Falsifier: a topology of this kind performs no better than shuffled addresses, or cannot recover a range-like response manifold. This tests whether anchors and coarse-to-fine order preserve a known physical variable.
03
Marmoset vocal feedback
Self-generated vocalisations against A1 activity, in the Eliades & Wang 2008 setting. Falsifier: no held-out prediction beyond acoustic energy, onset and motor-corollary baselines. This tests active feedback rather than passive stimulus response.
04
Cetacean behaviour, without neurons
Build stable coda and song addresses from independently recorded populations, and test identity, context, overlap, matching, cultural change and cross-site stability. Falsifier: addresses fail to replicate under microphone, recorder, location or individual hold-out. This is useful topology engineering, and it is not a neural map.
05
Non-invasive cetacean physiology, if it is ever justified
Pair controlled acoustic presentation with auditory evoked potentials or other permitted non-invasive measures. Falsifier: no reproducible phrase-conditioned response after correcting for loudness, timing, movement and subject identity. Even success would establish prediction of a measured evoked signal, not cortical semantics. At every rung, the address has to beat raw acoustic features, a standard spectrogram embedding, a simple timing model and a randomly generated topology carrying equal information.
What this cannot become

Nothing in this proposal can establish that a coda or a song phrase has meaning. A predictive acoustic-to-neural map would not identify a referent, an intention or a semantic composition; it would be an encoding model, signal in and measured response out, and a good predictor is not a dictionary.

A locus, a phrase address or a neural coordinate is descriptive and never authoritative. In a whale application, a highly repeatable acoustic feature must not be promoted to a behavioural landmark without independent evidence, and an inferred neural association stays a hypothesis until it survives held-out subjects, temporal shuffles, decoys and search-corrected nulls.

The strongest defensible outcome is modest: a reproducible, topology-preserving description of vocal signals, plus a rigorously tested prediction of a particular neural measurement where such data exist. For cetaceans today the first object is feasible and the second is not established.

The marker ladder, unchanged

A claim takes the weakest marker present in it, and confidence cannot promote a marker: only evidence can. Interpretation and hypothesis cannot anchor anything.

What the demonstration contributes, and what it does not

Its 3300-byte primer expands deterministically to 45 statements, a 32-syllable adjacent-phoneme codebook and an axis order derived from variance over a population of 204 places; the landmarks, codebook and population together would cost 67,561 bytes to ship, 20.47× the seed, and none of it is transmitted. A question-selecting generator resolves any of the 204 places in a mean of 17.8 questions, against an information floor of log2(204) = 7.672.

Across 90 family-and-place comparisons the same place came out nearest in 96.7% strictly and 100% counting exact ties, against a chance rate of 16.7%, and address Hamming tracks known distance at rho 0.9286 against a shuffled null of +0.0002 (sd 0.2704). Those numbers validate a topology-preserving protocol. They are not evidence about whales, and they do not make a whale address meaningful.
