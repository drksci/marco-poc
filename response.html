What agent:// is missing

Roland R. Rodriguez Jr, “Agent Identity URI Scheme: Topology-Independent Naming and Capability-Based Discovery for Multi-Agent Systems”, arXiv:2601.14567v2 (v1 21 January 2026, v2 13 July 2026), arxiv.org/abs/2601.14567. The paper binds agent identity to a trust root, a hierarchical capability path and a sortable unique identifier, so a URI survives migration; discovery derives DHT keys from the capability path, so a query returns agents by what they do rather than where they are. It reports 100% capability coverage on 369 production tools, discovery F1 = 1.0 across 10,000 agents, migration-invariance proofs, and under 5 microseconds per operation, and invites feedback on “DHT incentive models and capability mapping service design”.

The paper solves who, and its identifier destroys where on purpose

The identifier is SHA-256 over the agent's capability set, so two agents whose sets differ by a single entry receive unrelated keys, and no threshold on those keys recovers a distance. That is correct for reference and for audit: an identifier that drifted with behaviour could not be used to name anything.

It is also why the scheme has nothing to say about location. Two agents doing nearly the same job have no distance between them at all, because strings have no metric: there is nothing to compare except equality.

The paper names this weakness in itself
Different organisations may describe equivalent capabilities with incompatible paths: /workflow/approval and /process/authorize mean nearly the same thing, and exact-path discovery fails the pair. Its own remedy is an external capability-mapping service, a second system to run, and a second source of truth that can drift from both sides it maps.

Two organisations, the same four jobs, one decoy

Every agent below carries an agent:// URI and, alongside it, a ⌁ address derived from the situation the job happens in rather than from the job's name. The two sides do not observe identical statements (each records a slightly different set), so equivalent jobs land near each other rather than identical, which is why this is not a tautology.

Underneath that table is the whole of the claim, so here are the five steps by hand.

01
The two URIs, as issued
acme issues agent://acme.com/workflow/approval/01h0000000000000000000000 and globex issues agent://globex.io/process/authorize/01h0000000000000000000000. Both carry a stable unique suffix and a hierarchical capability path, and the path segments differ in both positions.
02
The two addresses, off the situations
acme's approval agent is ⌁ no.ki.ni.ke.ru·to.ru.ki.ru.ta+va.ki.ra.ma.no~ko.so.mo and globex's authorize agent is ⌁ no.ki.ni.ke.ru·to.no.ki.ru.ta+va.ki.ra.ma.no~ko.so.mo. Both are 18 syllables, and neither was derived from the capability path.
03
The Hamming distance between them
The two addresses agree on 17 of 18 syllables and differ at syllable 7, where acme reads ru and globex reads no. Two phonemes differ, which is the recorded Hamming 2, against 24.0 for non-equivalent pairs.
04
Discovery by capability path on this pair
Exact-path discovery finds nothing for workflow/approval, and across the four queries it scores recall 1/4. The single hit is process/audit, which prefix-matches all five process/* paths on the globex side, so it returns process/authorize, process/compile, process/revert, process/review and the decoy process/migrate; one of the five happens to carry the audit job, which is a shared string prefix and not a meaning.
05
Discovery by nearness on the same four queries
Nearest address returns the equivalent job every time: 2 for approval to authorize, 3 for build to compile, 2 for rollback to revert and 4 for audit to review, so recall is 4/4 with no mapping table in the path. The decoy is returned as nearest 0 times.

Are the equivalents actually the near ones?

All twenty acme-to-globex pairs are measured, not just the four queries, so the equivalence is visible as geometry rather than as a lucky nearest neighbour. The picture below shows the same mechanic on the primer's six places: nothing about the layout is chosen by hand, it is a classical MDS embedding of the address distance matrix, and address Hamming tracks the known ordering at Spearman rho = 0.9286 against a shuffled null of +0.0002 (sd 0.2704).

01
The four equivalent pairs
workflow/approval→process/authorize at 2, workflow/build→process/compile at 3, workflow/rollback→process/revert at 2, process/audit→process/review at 4. Sum 2 + 3 + 2 + 4 = 11, and 11 / 4 = 2.75. Range 2–4.
02
The sixteen non-equivalent pairs
Every other acme-to-globex pairing, including the four pairs against the decoy: 17, 18, 19, 20, 21, 23, 23, 24, 26, 26, 27, 27, 27, 27, 29, 30. Sum 384, and 384 / 16 = 24.0. Range 17–30.
03
The separation
24.0 − 2.75 = +21.25 syllables. No equivalent pair is closer than 2, and no non-equivalent pair is closer than 17, so the two populations do not touch.
04
The control, and the decoy
Shuffling which globex agent does which job and repeating the query 2,000 times gives recall 0.1994, sd 0.223, against the chance rate of one in five, 0.200; the real recall is 1.000. The decoy was returned as nearest 0 times.

The exact half: the root says same

The root is SHA-256 over a canonical serialisation of the statement set, under the single commitment rule commit(statements, extra) in marco/locus.py. Five of the six independently-built families produced the byte-identical root for locked_storeroom, ee9953d9b356615f308d8a6a4debc4578f17770cf6db6dac52d4f57ac13ad971, and five of the six again for dark_empty_room.

Across all six places, 20 of 36 model-place pairs fall in the single largest agreeing group, or 55.6%. Where the statement sets differ, the roots are unrelated: there is nothing to threshold and nothing to tune.

05
What that buys a registry
A registry holding only the root, the statement vocabulary and the commitment rule can recompute it from a published set and compare: 1 distinct output over 50 pinned runs, 1 over 50 unpinned runs, and 1 across fresh interpreters, which matched the in-process value. The primer is 3300 bytes and the frame it derives is 67,561 bytes, 20.47× the seed, and none of that frame is transmitted. Same bytes means the same situation was described, with no shared vocabulary, no mapping table and no third party.

Where the address has to stop

The address is descriptive and never authoritative: it must not absorb identity, trust, routing, or any of the paper's five requirements. agent:// is untouched, and the two objects ride alongside it and decide nothing on their own.

agent:// says who you are; ⌁ says whereabouts you are; the distance between two addresses says how close; the root says whether it is the same.

What is demonstrated here, and what is not

Demonstrated: an acme-to-globex query resolves to the equivalent job by nearness alone, with no mapping table anywhere in the path, at recall 4/4 against 1/4 for exact-path discovery; a root verifies without contacting the issuer, since 5 of 6 families produced the byte-identical root for locked_storeroom and any holder of the vocabulary can recompute it; the frame is usable by six independently-built model families; precision is variable and measured, where one axis names 3 of the 6 places, three axes name 4, four axes name 5, and six axes name all 6, at which point ⌁ ma.mo.ma already separates them; and identity and location are separated in code, with the commitment destroying adjacency on purpose.

Not demonstrated: that nearness resolves the capability-mapping weakness in a production federation. Two organisations with four hand-written situations is a demonstration, not a federation, the equivalent pairs were written as situations rather than observed from live traffic, and the four queries are run as a table rather than through a live discovery call. Both sides here derive their coordinates from the same primer, and a pair of organisations each holding its own frame is not measured.

The shared-vocabulary caveat travels with all of it: every family was handed the same 45 statements and agreed on 88.8% of its choices, and that agreement is what the addresses inherit. The framing result underneath it is measured: across 90 family-and-place comparisons the same place came out nearest in 96.7% strictly and 100% counting exact ties, against a chance rate of 16.7%, and address Hamming tracks known distance at Spearman rho 0.9286 against a shuffled null of +0.0002 (sd 0.2704). If nearness needed a curated mapping to work, the four queries here would have needed one, and no mapping table is in the path.

Most of this is older than it looks
prior art, named

Geohash and proquints make short pronounceable identifiers; locality-sensitive hashing preserves neighbourhoods; Merkle commitments give exact, order-independent equality; metric embeddings and canonical correlation analysis map between spaces; RSA is decades old.

The narrow claim is the conjunction: pronounceable and Gray-coded and locality-preserving, plus independently-built model families producing the same address and the same root. The novelty is in the join and in the measurement, not in any one part.
